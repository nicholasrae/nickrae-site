The right fit

What you’ll work on.

For pilots adding multi-engine privileges, especially those training in a PA-44 Seminole environment. It is intended for ground study and oral preparation alongside the flight training required for the add-on.

PA-44-focused multi-engine study. Aircraft-specific speeds, limitations, procedures and performance must come from your actual POH/AFM, approved checklists and instructor; a study workbook cannot set them for your aircraft.

  1. 01

    V-speeds and systems

    Organize the speeds and systems you need to explain, and connect them to your actual airplane’s documents.

  2. 02

    Vmc and asymmetric thrust

    Review the concepts and factors discussed during multi-engine training without treating a demonstration as an unsupervised exercise.

  3. 03

    One-engine-inoperative operations

    Ground-study prompts for engine-out procedures, single-engine decisions and OEI approaches.

  4. 04

    Performance and judgment

    Prepare to discuss runway, conditions and aircraft performance with your instructor rather than relying on a generic rule.

  5. 05

    Add-on oral preparation

    PA-44 Seminole framing and commercial-add-on knowledge review, as described in the author’s launch article.

This topic overview follows the author’s published Multi-Engine launch description. It does not supply operating limitations, memory items or an aircraft checklist.

One useful study session

Give the question some work.

  1. Put the correct POH beside the workbook

    Identify the airplane and document revision used in your training. Mark where an answer needs aircraft-specific information.

  2. Explain before rehearsing

    Discuss systems and performance on the ground. Learn and practice actual procedures through your instructor’s training plan.

  3. Debrief the differences

    Write down what changed with conditions or configuration and which source settles the answer. Use those notes in your next oral-review session.

Keep the current sources beside you.

Use this workbook with your instructor, the applicable FAA Airman Certification Standards, current FAA handbooks and your aircraft’s POH/AFM.
