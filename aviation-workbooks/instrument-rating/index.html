Look before you buy

Try the dual-VOR question.

One small exercise from the workbook, with its original answer and a clearly bounded source check.

Exercise 2.6 · item 2 only

2. You perform a dual VOR check. VOR #1 reads 271° and VOR #2 reads 268°. Is this acceptable?

☐ Yes ☐ No — The difference is ______°. Maximum allowed: ______°.

Show the workbook answer

2) Difference is 3°. Maximum 4°. Yes, acceptable.

Source context: 14 CFR §91.171(c) permits a maximum 4° variation for two VOR systems independent except for their antenna, tuned to the same ground facility. The 3° difference fits that limit. This comparison alone does not establish airworthiness or authorize a flight.

Regulatory context checked October 13, 2026 for this excerpt only. This is not a full-book review or a CFII endorsement.

One useful study session

Give the question some work.

  1. Answer before revealing

    Work a small set of questions without the key. Write the applicable limit and the conditions that make it relevant.

  2. Open the controlling source

    Check current regulations, the AIM, charts and your actual aircraft documents. A remembered number is only useful in the right context.

  3. Change one assumption

    Talk through how a different weather picture, equipment status or clearance changes your answer. Use that discussion with your CFII.

Keep the current sources beside you.

Use this workbook with your instructor, the applicable FAA Airman Certification Standards, current FAA handbooks and your aircraft’s POH/AFM. Published study examples do not replace current regulations, approved procedures or actual flight instruction. No FAA endorsement or checkride result is promised.
