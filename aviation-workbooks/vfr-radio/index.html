The right fit

What you’ll work on.

For student pilots building comfort on frequency, and returning pilots who want structured VFR radio practice. Use the drills on the ground and discuss local procedures and phrasing with your instructor.

VFR communications practice: call-building, non-towered and towered airport exchanges, traffic advisories, readbacks and recovery from missed information. It is a training aid, not live ATC instruction.

  1. 01

    Radio basics

    The core call structure, full and abbreviated call signs, numbers, frequencies and asking for a repeat.

  2. 02

    Airport conversations

    Non-towered airport calls and towered Class D exchanges, followed by busier arrival and sequencing exercises.

  3. 03

    Beyond the pattern

    VFR flight-following practice and Class C / Class B-style drills.

  4. 04

    Readback and recovery

    Readback Gym, recovery and emergency-practice sections, and listening tools.

  5. 05

    Repeatable page structure

    Situation, call-building fields, model exchange, readback trap and a variation; selected sections use their own prompt format.

Ground practice · simulated exchange

Build the call, then vary it.

The page leaves room to identify facility, aircraft, position, ATIS, request and notes. Its variation asks you to rewrite the call for a southbound departure.

Read the drill as text

Situation: You are on the ground practicing the five-part call structure.

Script skeleton / pilot: “Merced Ground, Cessna One Two Three Four Five, north ramp, taxi for VFR departure to the west, with Information Alpha.”

ATC: “Cessna One Two Three Four Five, taxi to Runway Three Zero via Alpha, hold short Runway Three Zero.”

Pilot readback: “Taxi to Runway Three Zero via Alpha, hold short Runway Three Zero, Cessna One Two Three Four Five.”

Readback trap: Do not start with a life story. Facility, call sign, position, request, clarifier. Then stop talking.

Variation: Rewrite for a southbound departure.

This is the book’s simulated practice exchange, not a current Merced clearance, airport diagram or taxi instruction. Use current charts, the AIM, local procedures, actual ATC instructions and your instructor for real operations.

One useful study session

Give the question some work.

  1. Read the situation

    Identify who you are calling, who you are, where you are and what you want. Use the page’s fields to prepare a concise call.

  2. Speak both sides of the practice exchange

    Read the model call and response, then say the pilot’s reply. Slow down when you lose clarity.

  3. Use the variation

    Change the detail requested on the page, write your version and repeat it. Confirm real airport details and procedures from current sources before any flight.

Keep the current sources beside you.
