Look before you buy

A diversion problem, with the reasoning visible.

Work out the time and fuel before revealing the answer. Then ask which assumptions make the arithmetic useful.

Read the question as text

You need to divert to an alternate airport 30 NM away. Your groundspeed is 100 knots, and you have 8 gallons of fuel remaining burning 8 GPH. Can you make it with VFR reserves?

  1. Yes — you'll arrive with plenty of fuel
  2. No — you won't have enough for the 30-minute VFR day fuel reserve
  3. Yes — you'll arrive in 18 minutes with about 5.6 gallons remaining, which gives more than 30 minutes reserve
  4. There's no way to calculate this
Reveal the answer and calculation

The key selects C. At the stated groundspeed, 30 NM takes 18 minutes. At 8 GPH, that uses 2.4 gallons, leaving 5.6 gallons: 42 minutes at the stated burn rate.

Context for this sample: that exceeds the airplane’s 30-minute daytime VFR reserve, but falls short of the 45-minute nighttime reserve. 14 CFR §91.151(a) also requires considering wind and forecast weather. The exercise assumes usable fuel and unchanged groundspeed and fuel burn; real planning needs the actual aircraft data and a margin beyond the minimum.

Source check for this calculation and reserve context: October 13, 2026. This is a study example, not a flight plan or a full-book technical review.

One useful study session

Give the question some work.

  1. Pick one weak area

    Choose a topic from your current lessons. Write down what you know before opening a source or an answer key.

  2. Explain the assumptions

    Say why the answer works and name what could change it. The diversion example above depends on usable fuel, wind, burn rate and day versus night.

  3. Close the loop with your instructor

    Bring uncertain answers to your instructor, check the controlling FAA or aircraft source, and revisit the question after a few days.

Keep the current sources beside you.

Use this workbook with your instructor, the applicable FAA Airman Certification Standards, current FAA handbooks and your aircraft’s POH/AFM. Published study examples do not replace current regulations, approved procedures or actual flight instruction. No FAA endorsement or checkride result is promised.
