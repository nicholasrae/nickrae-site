Best Student Pilot Apps for 2026: Compare by Task

Use what your school already includes, then fill one training gap. Compare written-test practice, oral preparation, flight planning, calculations, debriefing, and recordkeeping before adding a paid app.

Start with what is already included. Ask your instructor which question bank, planning tools, calculator, and recordkeeping system your school uses. A second app is useful when it fills a specific gap; it can also duplicate something you have paid for.

This guide covers U.S. private-pilot airplane training. Someone working toward the written test needs different practice from someone who can choose the right answer but struggles to explain it aloud. Identify that task before comparing subscriptions or buying a tablet.

Written-test practice: choose the exact ASA product

If your course already includes questions with explanations and practice tests, use those before buying a second bank. When you need another option, “ASA Prepware” refers to several different purchases:

  • Prepware Download: Private Pilot (PW-P) installs on Mac or PC and works without internet after installation. ASA also includes 24 months of Prepware Online access.
  • Prepware Online (PWO-STU) provides 24 months of browser access to one chosen knowledge-test database. Select Private Pilot for the relevant training goal.
  • Prepware Private Pilot mobile app: separate iPhone/iPad and Android listings support study on a phone or tablet. ASA describes its mobile apps as supplements and warns they do not have all the desktop software's features.

Check which access code came with your course or book, the current product updates, and whether you need offline study. Use practice results to choose topics to revisit with your instructor. Do not treat a question bank as a copy of the actual exam or a substitute for learning the subject.

Oral preparation: practice explaining a decision

Recognizing a written answer and explaining a flight decision are different tasks. Pick a scenario from your checkride prep plan, answer aloud, and show where you would verify the answer. Bring unanswered questions to your instructor.

For extra repetitions, compare the AI checkride prep tools by supported certificate, source references, voice access, and usage limits. AI feedback is practice material to check against current references and your instructor; a score does not establish readiness or provide an endorsement.

If the difficulty is forming a radio call, use the free ATC radio-practice comparison to distinguish free study content from a limited trial. The broader radio-practice directory includes other options. Check the individual product's current platform and microphone requirements.

Flight planning: check ForeFlight's device and plan first

Choose the EFB workflow with your instructor before buying hardware. ForeFlight Mobile supports iPhone and iPad, not Android. ForeFlight on the Web is a desktop planning workflow, not an Android cockpit app.

The current individual plan comparison lists Starter, Essential, and Premium. Starter includes core planning, weather, charts, weight and balance, and logging. Features such as synthetic vision and more advanced performance planning belong to higher plans. Match the required feature and geographic coverage to your lessons rather than assuming a higher tier is necessary.

Before using an EFB in the airplane, work through downloads, data currency, aircraft setup, power, mounting, and a backup plan with your instructor. A subscription alone does not confirm the device is ready for a flight. For the directory's source links and review status, see the ForeFlight listing.

Flight calculations: distinguish an app from a test-day tool

An E6B-style calculator can help with time, speed, distance, wind, and fuel problems. First check the calculator you already own and the functions included in your study software. Learn the inputs and units with your instructor, then check the result against the airplane's current data where applicable.

ASA’s CX-3 App is listed for iPhone/iPad and Android. The handheld CX-3 Flight Computer is a separate hardware purchase. ASA explicitly says the app is not allowed for knowledge-test taking. Do not assume a phone app has the same test-day permission as the handheld.

Debriefing: buy analysis only if you will review it

Begin with a specific question from the lesson: where did the track change, and what was happening at that point? Review an existing EFB recording with your instructor before adding another service.

CloudAhoy's published plans include a free Track account, with basic flight tracking, and paid Standard and Pro subscriptions. Standard adds debriefing analysis and visualization; automatic scoring is a Pro feature. Its iOS app and browser access serve different parts of the workflow. Current support guidance says there is no Android app.

Check that your recording or imported data works for the review you want. A GPS track and an automated score do not capture every cockpit action or decision. Use them to guide a discussion with your instructor, not as a proficiency assessment on their own.

Recordkeeping: a paid logbook is optional

If your EFB already includes a logbook, compare that with your needs first. For a separate free option, MyFlightbook offers browser, iOS, and Android access. Its published features include instructor signatures and endorsements; these were not independently tested for this article.

Agree with your instructor on how lessons and endorsements will be recorded. Check export formats, keep a backup you can access, and retain the signed source records you rely on. The first digital pilot logbook guide compares device support, free limits, and a migration checklist.

Keep the app budget tied to this week's lesson

  • No extra app spend: use included school resources and the FAA's downloadable aviation handbooks, including the Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge and Airplane Flying Handbook. Check the linked addenda too.
  • One missing task: choose a question bank, oral-practice tool, calculator, or EFB for that gap. Check the exact device, access period, renewal terms, and included features before paying.
  • An existing subscription: review its logging and track-review features before adding separate recordkeeping or debriefing software.

Try the chosen tool in one repeatable routine: prepare a question before the lesson, use the agreed planning workflow, then record the lesson and one next action afterward. If the tool adds more administration than useful practice, reassess it before renewal.

Student pilot app FAQ

What apps do student pilots actually need first?

Check what your school and ground-school course already include. Choose an extra tool for a specific gap: knowledge-test questions, oral practice, flight planning, calculations, debriefing, or recordkeeping. There is no single buying order for every student.

Does ForeFlight work on Android?

ForeFlight Mobile runs on iPhone and iPad, not Android. ForeFlight on the Web provides a separate desktop planning workflow; web access does not make the mobile cockpit app Android-compatible.

Is ASA Prepware Online the same as the mobile app?

No. Prepware Online provides browser-based access to a selected knowledge-test database. Prepware Download: Private Pilot is desktop software with online access included. The separate Prepware Private Pilot mobile app is a supplemental study tool with different features.

What is the best app for private pilot checkride prep?

Choose practice for the task you need to improve. Knowledge-test questions help with written preparation; oral preparation needs spoken explanations, aircraft-specific scenarios, source review, and instructor feedback. A practice score or AI answer does not establish checkride readiness.

Do student pilots need a paid digital logbook?

A paid logbook is optional. MyFlightbook offers free web, iOS, and Android access, and an existing EFB subscription may already include logging. Check instructor signatures, export, and backup needs before choosing a recordkeeping workflow.
