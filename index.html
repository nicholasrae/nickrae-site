Study aid only · October 2026

The scenario

You are planning a daytime VFR flight in a piston airplane. After accounting for forecast winds, your calculations show fuel to the first intended landing plus 35 minutes at normal cruising speed. Your passenger wants to leave now. Is meeting the minimum enough to make this a good plan?

Rule

Under 14 CFR 91.151(a), you may not begin a VFR airplane flight unless, considering wind and forecast weather, you have fuel to the first point of intended landing and then at least 30 minutes by day or 45 minutes at night, assuming normal cruising speed. In the stated daytime case, 35 minutes exceeds that particular minimum; it is not a complete go/no-go decision.

Risk

A small planning margin can disappear with stronger headwinds, delays, a diversion, or inaccurate usable-fuel and fuel-burn assumptions. Legal minimums are not recommended targets.

Action

Verify usable fuel and aircraft-specific performance with the applicable POH/AFM, choose a larger personal reserve, and identify an early fuel-stop or diversion trigger. Add fuel or shorten the leg when the margin is inadequate.

Follow-up reps

What changes for a night VFR airplane flight?
The planning reserve becomes at least 45 minutes at normal cruising speed after reaching the first intended landing.

Does a 35-minute daytime reserve guarantee a safe flight?
No. It answers only this fuel-minimum question. Conditions, performance assumptions, diversion options, and personal minimums still matter.

Common trap

Reciting 30/45 without accounting for winds and forecast weather, or treating the reserve as fuel you intend to use.

Source: 14 CFR 91.151(a). Source text checked October 13, 2026. Recheck current rules with your CFI.
